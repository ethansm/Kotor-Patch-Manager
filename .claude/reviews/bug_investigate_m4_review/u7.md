## Conclusions supported / overreaching
- Overreach: "the hotbar case gets a HIT at rank 1 every time" (note :38). HIT is mechanism-only on one bundle, from a single-member log.
- Overreach: "the blind Haiku roles put an identifiable partial or full mechanism in about 56% to 64%" (note :38). PARTIAL can be one keyword, and no CI is given.
- Overreach: "on 28 non-random units it removed 2 of 79 hypotheses, both MISS, and kept every HIT it saw" (note :39). The verified denominator is 28, so it is 2 of 28. Zero of 8 verified HITs rejected gives a 95% upper bound of 0.32 on false rejection.
- Overreach: "2 correct rejections" (note :27, :44). A rubric MISS is not truth, so "correct" is unmeasured.
- Overreach: "null abstains correctly" (note :43). Null said insufficient 10 of 10 with no control, which is saturation, not correctness.
- Supported as default only: "D3 stays 'layer 3 off'" (note :44). No measured benefit and about 10x unit cost, but 35% coverage and a known cap defect mean it is not a measured decision.
- Partly supported: "Go for M5" (note :43). Completion and cost hold; the quality claim rests on circular rubric labels.

## Recommendations
1. Freeze both rubrics, then hand-label at least 20 fresh hypotheses with another agent or the user; report kappa and do not edit the rubric on that set.
2. Cheapest extra metric ($0, seconds, from existing report.json): rescore with the symptom sentence and quoted evidence removed plus a negation window, and report the HIT delta per case.
3. Report per-run proportions with a run-cluster CI, a per-unit_id (style) breakdown, the all-label rate, and count None-top runs as misses.
4. Assert the scorer top_A/top_B equals report ranked[0], use the producer rank key, and report how often ties decide rank-1.
5. Before M5 or D3 decisions, add 1-2 not-a-bug control bundles and rerun verify with a cap covering all units (or a random sample).
